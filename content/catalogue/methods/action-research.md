Action Research is a participatory research methodology in which practitioners and researchers collaborate to address real-world problems through iterative cycles of planning, acting, observing, and reflecting. Rooted in the work of Kurt Lewin in the 1940s and later expanded by scholars such as Chris Argyris, Donald Schon, and Peter Reason, the approach treats research participants as co-investigators rather than subjects. Action Research bridges the gap between theory and practice by generating actionable knowledge that is both locally relevant and contributes to broader scholarly understanding. It is widely used in education, healthcare, social work, and community development. It is particularly relevant in Education, Social Sciences and Healthcare.

Action Research supports participatory, co-design and interdisciplinary collaboration and is suited for small teams, organizational settings and community-scale initiatives in in-person and hybrid settings.

Action Research is classified as a well-documented method, indicating broad adoption and available documentation. The Action Research method involves a structured process including: Diagnose: identify and define the problem collaboratively with stakeholders in the practice setting., Plan Action: design an intervention or change strategy based on the diagnosis and relevant theory., Take Action: implement the planned intervention in the real-world practice context., and further steps.
